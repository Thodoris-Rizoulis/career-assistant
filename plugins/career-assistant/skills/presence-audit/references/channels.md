# Channels by profession

Check only the channels that matter for the user's field and goal. More channels is not better: two strong ones beat five half-finished ones.

| Field | Channels that usually matter |
|---|---|
| Almost everyone | LinkedIn, a search of their name |
| Healthcare, law, accounting, engineering, teaching, trades | LinkedIn; the official professional register or licensing body listing (is the registration public and current?) |
| Design, architecture, photography, writing, media | A portfolio site, Behance or Dribbble, Instagram used professionally |
| Software, data, IT | GitHub or GitLab, a personal site or blog, Stack Overflow |
| Research and academia | Google Scholar, ORCID, ResearchGate, the university profile page |
| Sales, marketing, consulting, business | LinkedIn above all: posts, recommendations, featured work |
| Freelance and independent | A website with services and testimonials, freelance platform profiles (Upwork, Malt, Fiverr), Google Business Profile for local work |
| Creators and educators | YouTube, a blog or newsletter, course platforms |

## LinkedIn

- Headline: says the role and the value, in the words employers search for (not just the current job title).
- About: first person, 3 short paragraphs: what they do, proof, what they're open to.
- Experience: results, not duties; the same facts as the CV.
- Featured section: the best proof (case study, certificate, article, project).
- Skills: the goal's key requirements near the top.
- Recommendations: 2–3 recent ones that name specific skills.
- Open to work / services settings match the goal.

## Portfolio or personal website

- Can a visitor tell in 5 seconds who this is and what they do?
- Work is shown with context and results (the problem, what they did, what changed), not just pictures.
- Easy contact; working links to LinkedIn, CV, and other profiles.
- Loads fast and works on a phone.
- Information matches the CV.

## GitHub

- Profile README: who they are, what they do, what they're looking for.
- 4–6 pinned repos that match the goal.
- Important repos have a README with what it does, why, a screenshot or demo, how to run it, and the tech used.
- Many empty, tutorial, or half-finished repos make the profile look messy: suggest archiving or making them private.
- The languages and tools shown match the goal.

## Professional registers and licensing bodies

- Is the user listed, with the right name and current status?
- If the register is public, link to it from LinkedIn or the CV where that's normal in the field.

## Research profiles

- Google Scholar and ORCID list all publications and are linked to each other.
- The research focus in the bio matches the goal.

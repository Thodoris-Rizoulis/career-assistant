# CV writing guide

Recruiters spend well under a minute on a first read. Every line should show **what the person did and what changed because of it**. This works the same in every profession.

## The highlight formula

**Action verb + what you did + result (with a number when possible)**

Weak → Strong:

- "Responsible for patient care in the ICU" → "Cared for 2–3 critical patients per shift in a 14-bed ICU; zero medication errors in 2 years"
- "Handled month-end closing" → "Cut month-end close from 8 to 5 working days by automating 3 reconciliation reports"
- "Taught maths to secondary students" → "Taught maths to 120 students a year; pass rate on national exams rose from 71% to 84%"
- "Managed key accounts" → "Grew 12 key accounts by 30% in revenue in one year, 118% of quota"
- "Worked on performance improvements" → "Cut page load time from 3.2s to 1.1s by adding caching"
- "Helped new colleagues" → "Trained 6 new team members; all passed probation"

## Good kinds of numbers

People served (customers, patients, students, users), money (revenue, budget, cost saved), time (saved, faster, deadlines met), volume (cases, projects, transactions), quality (errors, complaints, ratings, pass rates), team size, growth (%), rankings (top 10%, 1st of 20). Estimates are fine if marked honestly ("~", "about").

## Strong action verbs

Led, managed, built, created, designed, launched, improved, increased, reduced, cut, grew, delivered, trained, mentored, organized, negotiated, introduced, streamlined, resolved, won.

Avoid starting with: "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped with", "Involved in", "Participated in", "Duties included".

A strong verb must still be true. Use "led" only if the person led it; "co-led" or "delivered with one other developer" if they shared it. Use "mentored" only for real mentoring, not just reviewing someone's work. When unsure, ask.

## Words to avoid

Empty claims anyone can write: "hard-working", "team player", "passionate", "detail-oriented", "results-driven", "dynamic", "go-getter". Show these through highlights instead.

## Summary (2–3 lines)

Role + years of experience + main strengths + the kind of work they're best at, pointed at the goal. Plain and specific.

Examples:
- "ICU nurse with 6 years of experience in cardiac and post-surgical care. Trusted with training new staff and leading the unit's sepsis protocol."
- "Accountant with 5 years in audit and month-end reporting for manufacturing companies. Strong at automating slow reporting in Excel and Power BI."

## Skills section

- Group into 3–6 labels, named the way the profession thinks.
- Strongest and most relevant first.
- Leave out outdated or basic items unless the job asks for them.
- Don't rate skills with bars or stars.

## Style

- Short lines. Most highlights fit on one line, never more than two.
- No "I" or "my" in highlights.
- Past tense for past roles, present tense for the current role.
- Consistent formatting for dates, numbers, and names of tools, certificates, and organizations.
- Spell names the way the job ad and the official source spell them.

## For CV-scanning software (ATS)

- Single column, standard section names (Experience, Education, Skills).
- Plain text for important information. Don't hide keywords in images or icons.
- Include the exact names of relevant skills, tools, certificates, and licenses, since scanners often match on words.

## Conventions by country and profession

These are common expectations, not strict rules. If unsure for the user's target country or field, search for current advice ("CV format <country> <year>").

| Market | Common expectations |
|---|---|
| US, Canada | Called a résumé. 1 page early in a career, 2 at most. No photo, date of birth, or marital status. |
| UK, Ireland | Up to 2 pages. No photo or date of birth. |
| Germany, Austria, Switzerland | Often 2 pages, clear dates (month/year). A photo is still common but increasingly optional. Certificates matter. |
| Southern Europe (for example Greece, Italy, Spain) | A photo is common but optional. Europass is sometimes asked for in the public sector. |
| EU institutions and some public sector jobs | Europass format may be required. Check the job ad. |
| International or remote roles | Follow US/UK style: no photo, results first, 1–2 pages. |

| Profession | Common expectations |
|---|---|
| Academia and research | Longer CVs are normal: publications, grants, teaching, conferences. No page limit. |
| Healthcare | Licenses, registrations, and certifications near the top, with numbers and expiry dates where relevant. |
| Law, accounting, engineering | Professional qualifications and admissions near the top. |
| Design and creative | A clean CV plus a portfolio link near the top; the portfolio does most of the work. |
| Trades | Licenses, certificates, and safety training near the top; practical results. |

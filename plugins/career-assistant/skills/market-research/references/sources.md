# Where to look

Sites change, close, or block automated access. Treat this as a starting list, not a fixed one. If a site doesn't load or looks outdated, move on and say so in the findings.

The best sources depend on the profession and the country. When in doubt, search for "<profession> jobs <country>", "<profession> salary survey <country> <year>", and "<profession> competency framework".

## Job ads (the best signal for what's wanted)

- **General job boards:** LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, Glassdoor, and national boards (for example kariera.gr in Greece, StepStone in Germany, Reed in the UK, Seek in Australia). LinkedIn may need a browser tool to read fully.
- **Employer careers pages:** many use hosted job boards (Workable, Greenhouse, Lever, Personio, SmartRecruiters) that are easy to read.
- **Profession-specific boards:** most fields have them. For example: healthcare (hospital group sites, national health service job sites), education (school networks, university job boards), public sector (national government job portals, EU institutions), creative (design and media job boards), tech (Hacker News "Who is hiring?", remote job boards), freelance (Upwork, Malt, Fiverr: useful for rates and demand).
- **Remote boards:** We Work Remotely, Remote OK, Welcome to the Jungle. Useful for "working remotely for a foreign employer".

How to sample: search the exact target title + market, open 10–20 ads from the last ~30 days, and record must-haves vs nice-to-haves for each.

## What separates the levels

- **Career and competency frameworks** published by professional bodies (for example nursing and medical councils, accounting bodies like ACCA or CPA associations, project management institutes, engineering chambers, teaching standards).
- **Public career ladders** that companies publish for their own staff.
- **Job ads for the next level up**, compared with ads for the current level.
- **Licensing and certification requirements** from official bodies.

## Pay

- **Pay ranges inside job ads:** the most reliable when present. EU pay-transparency rules are making them more common.
- **National statistics offices and government labour data** (for example Eurostat, national statistics services, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics).
- **Public-sector pay scales:** published and exact, for public jobs.
- **Salary surveys** from recruitment agencies (Hays, Michael Page, Robert Walters, and others) and from professional bodies.
- **Self-reported sites:** Glassdoor, Payscale, Levels.fyi (strong for big tech, weak elsewhere). Wide coverage, but noisy.
- **Freelance rates:** freelance platforms and professional association rate surveys.

Always record: role, level, location, currency, gross or net, per year or per month, and the source date.

## Trends (use carefully)

- Yearly reports from recruitment agencies and professional bodies.
- Large surveys with a clear method (for example national skills surveys, the World Economic Forum Future of Jobs report).
- News and blog posts: useful context, but often hype. Prefer the job ad data.

## Trust ranking (highest first)

1. Requirements and pay ranges in real, recent job ads; official licensing requirements
2. Official statistics, frameworks, and surveys with a clear method and a large sample
3. Recruitment agency salary guides
4. Self-reported salary sites
5. Articles and opinion pieces
